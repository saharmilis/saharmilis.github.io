## About

Bias is everywhere; it is overt and unmistakable, but more often it is subtle and hidden.

In the preprocessing phase of large texts, standard flat deduplication creates severe gaps by systematically wiping out the subtle variations of minority dialects, heavily favoring the high-volume dominant language variant.

Failing to account for dialectal differences at this stage risks erasing minoritized grammatical structures and localized semantic nuances before pretraining begins.

This research explores **integrating dialectal bias classifiers directly into the deduplication step** to distinguish redundant text from meaningful dialectal variation. The goal is a data-cleaning approach that protects linguistic diversity and prevents the dominant language variant from erasing minority dialects.

By using dialect classifiers during preprocessing, we shift the goal from blind text reduction to intentional diversity preservation, working toward a fairer and more representative corpus before the model ever begins to learn.
